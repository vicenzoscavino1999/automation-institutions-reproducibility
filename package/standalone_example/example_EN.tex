\begin{abstract}
This document isolates the worked quantitative example that accompanies
\emph{Automation, Institutions, and Economic Freedom}. It joins a liquid-income
displacement, a fiscal collection rule, household incidence, public allocation,
institutional capacity, and a future opportunity service. The exercise has two
dates and two household groups. It measures direct expenditure at fixed prices;
it is not a solved HANK equilibrium, an empirical calibration, or an estimate.
Every displayed table and every plotted coordinate is generated from the
machine-readable output of one verified run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, timing, and scope}

The example asks when a common automation shock and public budget can pass two
different gates:
\[
\Delta D_0^{\mathrm{dir}}\geq 0,
\qquad
\text{date-1 opportunity}\geq \OO_{\min}.
\]
The first gate records immediate incidence and first-round expenditure. The
second requires both installed capacity and a funded operating input. Separating
the gates makes it possible for a policy to pass one, both, or neither.

There is a unit mass of households. Group $H$ is exposed to the automation
income shift and group $S$ owns the corresponding claim. Group variables are
aggregates. At date 0, a liquid-income flow $Z>0$ moves from $H$ to $S$.
At date 1, a public reserve may finance an operating input. Prices and the gross
return on that reserve are normalized to
\[
P_0=P_1=R_B=1.
\]
The effective run configuration closes the baseline household accounts as
follows:
\[
\input{tables/household_closure.tex}
\]
The spending coefficients are local affine responses over the finite synthetic
allocations studied here, not global consumption functions. In particular,
initial consumption is not obtained by multiplying initial income by a marginal
spending response.

\section{Fiscal capture and closed accounts}

Let $\tau$ be the statutory rate, $\lambda_{\mathrm{legal}}$ legal coverage, and
$\chi_{\mathrm{comp}}$ compliance. Gross receipts are
\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{legal}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{legal}}Z,
\qquad
T=\tau\chi_{\mathrm{comp}}B_{\mathrm{legal}}
=\kappa_{\mathrm{gross}}Z,
\qquad
\kappa_{\mathrm{gross}}=\tau\lambda_{\mathrm{legal}}\chi_{\mathrm{comp}}.
\end{equation}
The government pays an administrative cost $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and allocates the
remaining receipts across a transfer $y_T$, capacity investment $I_q$, a reserve
$B_0$, and an unused financial balance $U_0$:
\begin{align}
T&=C_{\mathrm{adm}}+y_T+I_q+B_0+U_0,\\
R_BB_0&=X_1+B_1.
\end{align}
All fiscal allocations are nonnegative:
\[
C_{\mathrm{adm}},y_T,I_q,B_0,U_0,X_1,B_1\geq0.
\]
The reserve is a public deposit asset with an equal depository liability. It is
not date-0 spending. Administrative and investment purchases enter the declared
first-round measure once; the later spending of their suppliers is outside that
measure.

Household income changes are
\[
Y_H=Y_{H0}-Z+y_T,
\qquad
Y_S=Y_{S0}+Z-T.
\]
With $m_T=m_H$, consumption and saving obey
\begin{align}
C_H&=C_{H0}+m_H(-Z+y_T),
&S_H&=S_{H0}+(1-m_H)(-Z+y_T),\\
C_S&=C_{S0}+m_S(Z-T),
&S_S&=S_{S0}+(1-m_S)(Z-T).
\end{align}
End-of-date liquid assets satisfy $A_{j1}=A_{j0}+S_j$. The implementation checks
nonnegative consumption and the applicable liquid-asset restrictions for every
reported allocation.

\section{The two gates}

\subsection{Direct demand}

Let $d_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and $d_I$ denote the portions of administrative and
investment purchases included in first-round domestic expenditure. The direct
demand change is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\DD=-m_HZ+m_S(Z-T)+m_Hy_T
+d_{\mathrm{adm}}C_{\mathrm{adm}}+d_II_q.}
\end{equation}
The demand gate passes when $\DD\geq0$. This is an incidence result under fixed
prices and fixed spending responses. An output or inflation result would require
an additional equilibrium closure.

\subsection{Capacity and opportunity}

Inherited capacity depreciates and may suffer damage:
\begin{align}
q_1^{\mathrm{pre}}&=(1-\delta_q)q_0-d_q,\\
q_1&=\Pi_{[0,1]}\!\left(q_1^{\mathrm{pre}}+\phi I_q\right).
\end{align}
Operating input and capacity are complements:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal S_1^O=\min\{aX_1,bq_1\},
\qquad
\OO_1=\OO_0+\rho_O\mathcal S_1^O.
\end{equation}
The opportunity gate passes when $\OO_1\geq\OO_{\min}$.

Define the service requirement
\[
s_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{[\OO_{\min}-\OO_0]_+}{\rho_O}.
\]
The minimum-input formulas maintain the primitive conditions
$a,b,\rho_O,\phi>0$.
For $0<s_{\mathrm{req}}\leq b$, the componentwise minima are
\begin{equation}
X_{\min}=\frac{s_{\mathrm{req}}}{a},\quad
q_{\min}=\frac{s_{\mathrm{req}}}{b},\quad
I_{\min}(\phi)=\frac{[q_{\min}-q_1^{\mathrm{pre}}]_+}{\phi},\quad
B_{\min}=\frac{X_{\min}}{R_B}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed transfer, the opportunity target is financeable exactly when
\begin{equation}
y_T+I_{\min}(\phi)+B_{\min}\leq T-C_{\mathrm{adm}}.
\end{equation}
If $s_{\mathrm{req}}=0$, the minima are zero. If $s_{\mathrm{req}}>b$, the target
is technically impossible because projected capacity cannot exceed one.

\section{Fixed-allocation frontiers}

Holding the fiscal allocation of C fixed gives
\[
T=20,\quad C_{\mathrm{adm}}=1,\quad y_T=12,\quad I_q=3,\quad B_0=X_1=4.
\]
The direct-demand expression reduces to
\[
\DD=4-8m_H,
\]
so demand passes exactly when
\begin{equation}
m_H\leq m_H^*=\frac12.
\end{equation}
Capacity is $q_1=\Pi_{[0,1]}(0.07+3\phi)$. Because the required capacity is
$0.8$, the opportunity gate passes exactly when
\begin{equation}
\phi\geq\phi^*=\frac{73}{300}=0.243\overline{3}.
\end{equation}
Equality belongs to both passing regions.

\section{Allocation under the common fiscal envelope}

For a positive and technically attainable opportunity target, reserve the
minimum operating balance and set unused funds to zero. Let
\[
F=T-C_{\mathrm{adm}}-B_{\min}.
\]
When the productive-investment ceiling is imposed, investment is restricted to
the amount that can raise capacity to one. Substitution into direct demand gives
the affine objective
\[
 A_D=-m_HZ+m_S(Z-T)+d_{\mathrm{adm}}C_{\mathrm{adm}},
 \qquad
\DD=A_D+m_HF+(d_I-m_H)I_q.
\]

four capped vertices and three uncapped vertices establish the two maxima; the

\section{What the example establishes}

Within its declared closure, the example establishes five operational points.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fiscal capture can be decomposed into rate, coverage, and compliance.
\item Stabilizing current direct demand and producing future opportunity are
distinct objectives.
\item A common public envelope may pass one gate, both gates, or neither.
\item Installed capacity cannot replace a missing operating input.
\item The intersection of the two gates is nonempty under the exact conditional
frontiers shown above.
\end{enumerate}

The exercise does not solve the parent HANK model, determine the automation
income shift, endogenize prices, estimate parameters, or rank policies by social
welfare. Those tasks require the additional modules identified in the book.

\section{Reproduction contract}

The document-build directory contains the LaTeX source, six generated
tables, two vector PDF figures, a table manifest, a figure-validation record, and
a build report. The required order is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item execute a new reproduction run;
\item verify the completed run;
\item execute \texttt{build-docs} using that run and a new output directory;
\item inspect every rendered page.
\end{enumerate}

This sequence is autonomous. The package-only \texttt{coverage} command checks
the structure of the coverage registry without the manuscript. Two optional
correspondence checks---\texttt{coverage --manuscript} and \texttt{check-book}---
require an identified copy of manuscript v22 supplied separately. The absence of
that protected manuscript is not a failure of the standalone example.

The figures embed Bitstream Vera Sans from the pinned ReportLab dependency. The
font is redistributable under the Bitstream Vera Fonts Copyright notice included
with the package. Font substitution changes typography and PDF metadata, not the
economic coordinates or region definitions.
